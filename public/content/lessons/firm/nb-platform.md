## Firm Overview and Scale

Neuberger Berman is an independent, employee-owned investment management firm founded in 1939. The firm manages approximately **$390 billion** in total AUM. Its Private Markets platform has scaled aggressively, reaching **$177 billion** in AUM as of the 2025–2026 reporting cycle, up from $140–155 billion just a few years prior.

Deployment velocity underscores the platform's scale: in a single recent year the firm deployed **over $17.5 billion** across more than **300 transactions**. This is executed by a team of **450+ private market professionals** across **15–17 global offices** (key hubs: New York, London, Hong Kong). The firm's philosophy rejects short-term market timing in favor of dynamic, cross-cycle investing grounded in fundamental, bottom-up underwriting.

## Core Strategies

### Primaries (Fund Investments)

The primary fund investment platform manages **in excess of $40 billion** in commitments. It serves as the foundational intelligence engine: by securing capacity in oversubscribed funds, the primaries team builds deep GP relationships and captures granular, real-time data on performance, valuations, and proprietary deal flow. That intelligence directly informs co-investment underwriting and secondary pricing.

### Co-Investments — PIPCO

**Private Investment Portfolios and Co-Investments (PIPCO)** focuses on direct minority equity investments made alongside premier lead GPs. Key characteristics:

- **Fee efficiency**: co-investments typically carry reduced or zero management fee and carry drag.
- **Capital solutions**: NB acts as a bridge for GPs executing large transactions that would breach their own fund concentration limits.
- **Scale**: over **$40 billion** in dedicated co-investment capital.

Representative PIPCO portfolio names include Solenis (Industrials/Chemicals), Nord Anglia Education, Proofpoint (Cybersecurity), and Mavis Discount Tire. Co-investments target enterprise software, industrial manufacturing, and educational services.

### Secondaries (LP-Led and GP-Led)

The secondaries business manages **more than $25 billion** with a dual mandate:

1. **LP-led**: acquiring traditional LP interests in mature, cash-generative funds to mitigate the J-curve.
2. **GP-led continuation vehicles**: anchoring complex sponsor-initiated transactions that transfer high-conviction assets from older funds into new vehicles.

Transactions are privately negotiated rather than broad-auctioned, targeting attractive entry multiples. Recent vintage strategies concentrate approximately **90% of exposure in mature buyouts**.

### Private Credit and Specialty Strategies

Beyond equity, the platform extends into:
- Private debt and capital solutions
- European private loans
- Residential real estate finance
- Specialty finance

These provide yield-oriented alternatives for institutional allocators navigating volatile macro environments.

## The Dyal Capital → Blue Owl History

**Dyal Capital Partners** was the pioneer of the GP stakes strategy: acquiring minority equity in the management companies of alternative asset managers, securing rights to stable management fee streams and carry across all future funds. Dyal was founded, incubated, and scaled entirely within Neuberger Berman, raising Dyal Funds I through IV.

In **2021**, Dyal entered a definitive combination with **Owl Rock Capital Group** (direct lending) and **Altimar Acquisition Corporation** (SPAC) to form **Blue Owl Capital (NYSE: OWL)**. The **$12.5 billion transaction** generated approximately **$1.8 billion in gross proceeds** and provided partial liquidity to NB while listing the combined entity publicly.

**Result**: Neuberger Berman retained equity interests in Blue Owl but the direct, active management of mega-cap GP stakes is no longer a core internal strategy. Today the firm's GP stakes exposure is specialized, executed through the **Almanac real estate platform**, which acquires minority equity exclusively in growth-oriented real estate investment managers.

## Current Outlook Themes (1H 2026)

NB's "Private Markets Outlook 1H 2026: Balancing Opportunity and Complexity" identifies three structural themes:

### 1. Agentic AI

Beyond large language model training, autonomous AI agents are expected to **rebuild information architectures** across the software sector, creating massive dislocation and value-creation opportunities for nimble, software-focused buyout sponsors. Portfolio companies can achieve unprecedented EBITDA margin expansion by automating complex back-office workflows via agentic systems.

### 2. Backlog of Older Buyout Funds

NAV for buyout funds aged **seven years or older** reached **$863 billion** in late 2025. With IPO and M&A exit channels congested, GPs are compelled to use **GP-led continuation vehicles** as alternative liquidity mechanisms. NB's secondaries team (SOF VI) is explicitly positioned to anchor these transactions.

### 3. Expanding Investment Structures

As evergreen and ELTIF funds proliferate, NB urges wealth advisors to carefully analyze:
- **Portfolio-valuation frequencies** (how often NAV is struck)
- **Redemption mechanics** (lock-ups, gates, notice periods)

This structural complexity creates both risk and differentiation opportunity for sophisticated LP allocators.
